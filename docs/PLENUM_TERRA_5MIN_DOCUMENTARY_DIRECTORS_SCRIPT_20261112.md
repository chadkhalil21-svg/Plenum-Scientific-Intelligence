## Timed script and shot-level source gates

### Scene 01 — 00:00–00:30 | Opening — the pasture (57 words)
**Picture:** Real CC0 cattle/pasture footage; amber natural light; quiet introductory score.

**Narration:** “Every landscape tells a story. In a pasture, that story begins with grass, sunlight, soil, and the animals that depend upon them. Yet there is another part of the story we cannot see: gases produced during digestion. Among them is methane. Understanding where it comes from is the first step toward asking whether we can reduce it.”

**On-screen identification:** PLENUM TERRA — A Scientific Research Concept

**Source/rights gate:** FAO enteric methane; existing CC0 Commons cattle footage or individually cleared stock

### Scene 02 — 00:30–01:00 | The biology of methane (53 words)
**Picture:** Real cattle B-roll, original rumen microbe schematic; no false lab footage.

**Narration:** “Cattle and other ruminants digest fibrous plants with help from communities of microorganisms. As these organisms ferment feed in the rumen, methane is formed as a by-product. Much of it leaves the animal through belching. That process is natural, but it is also an important research target because the methane reaches the atmosphere.”

**On-screen identification:** Enteric fermentation — established biology

**Source/rights gate:** FAO; EPA enteric fermentation

### Scene 03 — 01:00–01:30 | Why it matters (57 words)
**Picture:** Original molecule graphic and abstract atmosphere visual; genuine pasture cutaways.

**Narration:** “Methane does not remain in the atmosphere as long as carbon dioxide, but it is a powerful greenhouse gas while it is there. Livestock methane therefore attracts scientific attention alongside other agricultural emissions. No single intervention can solve the climate challenge. Responsible progress means measuring the effects of particular interventions in real animals and real production systems.”

**On-screen identification:** Methane mitigation requires measured outcomes

**Source/rights gate:** FAO; avoid unsupported quantitative climate claims

### Scene 04 — 01:30–02:00 | A documented precedent (58 words)
**Picture:** Illustration of seaweed, not unlicensed real microscopy; original reaction-path diagram with general labels.

**Narration:** “One line of investigation has focused on compounds associated with the red seaweed Asparagopsis. Peer-reviewed cattle feeding studies have reported substantial reductions in enteric methane under specific experimental conditions. These are genuine published findings. They describe tested feed supplements, however, not a pasture grass engineered by Plenum. The distinction matters, and it shall remain visible throughout this film.”

**On-screen identification:** Published Asparagopsis feed-supplement research — not Plenum Terra results

**Source/rights gate:** Roque 2021; Cowley 2024

### Scene 05 — 02:00–02:30 | What remains uncertain (61 words)
**Picture:** Visual transition from paper pages to unlabeled original scientific chart; no invented plot data.

**Narration:** “A result in one feeding trial cannot be treated as a guaranteed result in every herd, diet, location, or delivery system. Scientists must examine dose, persistence, animal health, residues, environmental implications, and how an intervention performs over time. Each of those questions demands its own evidence. The research precedent is promising; the path to a dependable agricultural product is more demanding.”

**On-screen identification:** Efficacy, safety, residues and delivery require separate evaluation

**Source/rights gate:** Published trials; include counterevidence and safety considerations during review

### Scene 06 — 02:30–03:00 | The Plenum Terra idea (57 words)
**Picture:** Original forage CGI and stylized pasture; persistent concept overlay.

**Narration:** “Plenum Terra begins with a different research question: might a future forage-based approach be designed to bring a methane-mitigation pathway closer to the pasture itself? That is a research ambition, not an achievement announced here. The plants, biochemical mechanisms, containment strategies, and practical delivery would all require rigorous development and independent examination before anyone could claim success.”

**On-screen identification:** PLENUM TERRA — CONCEPT VISUALIZATION / NOT FIELD RESULTS

**Source/rights gate:** Founder-approved public concept only; no private implementation details

### Scene 07 — 03:00–03:30 | From idea to experiment (61 words)
**Picture:** Original nonliteral lab animations: controls, sample labels, analytical measurement icons.

**Narration:** “A credible answer starts with reproducible experiments. Researchers would first need to establish whether a proposed biological system can function as intended, then measure what it produces and how consistently it performs. Suitable controls, transparent measurements, and independent replication would matter at every stage. Attractive images cannot substitute for data, and a concept diagram must never be mistaken for experimental proof.”

**On-screen identification:** Proposed research sequence — no completed Terra study implied

**Source/rights gate:** Scientific method; project status concept only

### Scene 08 — 03:30–04:00 | Safety and agricultural reality (65 words)
**Picture:** Pasture images, grazing context, original icons for plant health and animal health.

**Narration:** “There is more to agricultural innovation than a promising laboratory signal. Any forage intended for livestock must be evaluated for animal safety, nutrition, agronomic performance, environmental effects, and applicable regulatory requirements. Researchers must also ask whether it can fit ordinary farming conditions. An intervention that cannot be used responsibly in the field cannot simply be declared a solution on the strength of an early mechanism.”

**On-screen identification:** Animal, plant, environmental and regulatory review required

**Source/rights gate:** Risk assessment framing; no authorization claims

### Scene 09 — 04:00–04:30 | What trustworthy progress looks like (63 words)
**Picture:** Split-screen original animations showing proposed phases; use 'planned' not 'completed'.

**Narration:** “If the scientific questions can be answered, the next stage would require careful controlled evaluation, independent review, and eventually appropriately authorized field research. Failures and unexpected results must be recorded as seriously as successes. The goal is not a dramatic percentage on a screen. It is knowledge strong enough to withstand scrutiny, and perhaps one day to support a genuinely useful agricultural tool.”

**On-screen identification:** Illustrative stages: feasibility → safety → controlled trials → independent review

**Source/rights gate:** Never portray proposed milestones as achieved

### Scene 10 — 04:30–05:00 | Closing — invitation to inquiry (58 words)
**Picture:** Return to real CC0 cattle footage; evening field; restrained title; original instrumental outro.

**Narration:** “Plenum Terra stands today as a scientific research concept inspired by evidence and constrained by questions still unanswered. Its value will depend on experiments, transparent reporting, collaboration, and the willingness to test assumptions. In the pasture, the future begins not with a promise of certainty, but with a question worth investigating. Plenum Terra: research rooted in the pasture.”

**On-screen identification:** PLENUM TERRA | Research rooted in the pasture | Concept under investigation

**Source/rights gate:** No claims of demonstrated Terra efficacy; no public release without founder approval
