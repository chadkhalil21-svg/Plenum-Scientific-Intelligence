## Executive direction
Adopt a **single accountable virtual film director**, not dozens of unmanaged competing cinematic agents. The founder sets intended audience, accuracy, film category, brand and budget. The existing Plenum Chief assigns the Creative Studio Director to produce one approved treatment, shot plan and continuity bible, coordinates work, and routes scientific/rights checks to independent roles. ## Production rubric and editorial hierarchy
1. **Genre + audience:** scientific documentary/educational explainer, theatrical science fiction, character narrative, institutional corporate film, commercial advertisement, vertical social short, tutorial, animated film, music video, or interview/podcast. One principal genre per deliverable.
2. **Creative brief:** central promise; audience; distribution (web/streaming/film festival/social); duration; aspect ratio; delivery resolution; visual thesis; sound identity; evidence classification; deadline; actual zero-cost runtime constraints.
3. **Script:** logline -> premise -> treatment -> beat sheet -> screenplay or timed two-column AV script (picture / voice-audio) -> source annotations. Write in **Fountain** for screenplays; use a timed shot table for documentaries and product films. The principal scientific point must not be submerged by generic promotional flourish.
4. **Direction:** director selects *one primary* visual language and a secondary accent: camera lens/framing, camera motion, lighting, color system, scene transition logic, subject continuity, typography, music dynamics, narration identity and ambient sound. Style must serve audience and factual status.
5. **Storyboards:** per shot specify ID, start/end, picture, source asset/license, narration, subtitles, motion/VFX, audio stems and claim source. Create a rough animatic before expensive effects.
6. **Production:** footage/illustrations -> edit first assembly -> score & Foley -> VFX/composite -> grade -> audio edit/master -> captions and transcript -> legal/scientific & usability review -> release approval.
7. **Review:** scientific accuracy, originality, continuity, licensing, accessible subtitles, comprehensible audio, proper contrast, genuine file playback, factual on-screen labels, iPhone-compatible H.264/AAC MP4. Review actual media, not only a green static test.

### Exclusions and restrictions
- **F5-TTS:** MIT code but official model weights CC-BY-NC; do not use stock weights in Plenum commercial films. https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS
- **BBC Sound Effects:** research/personal/educational access is not a blanket company commercial license. https://sound-effects.bbcrewind.co.uk/licensing
- **Suno/Udio free tiers:** no inferred commercial rights to generated songs.
- **GitHub-hosted actions:** event-driven public task automation, not unlimited free commercial rendering and inference infrastructure.
- **YouTube video soundtracks:** do not copy soundtracks out of videos; use original music or specifically licensed assets.
- **Synthetic voices:** choose consent-based or public provided voices; no impersonation of identifiable actors/celebrity narrators.
- **All voice/audio libraries:** model and code licenses do not automatically license third-party audio tracks, samples or performances.

## Voice quality control (solve prior robotic audio)
Narrator audition is **not** a general TTS installation check. Produce 12–20 seconds of the SAME approved public script with 2–3 legitimate candidates, then compare blinded A/B/C on naturalness, accent, intelligibility, chemistry pronunciation, expressive restraint, silence/pauses, sibilance, breaths, clipping and artifact count. Voice casting requires human acceptance, not a tool label. Scene-split long scripts into 1–3-sentence takes; use punctuation and speech pauses intentionally. Edit in room tone and mild ambience; duck music under words, avoid drowning weak TTS in a soundtrack. Publish only an approved speaker identity and audio rights provenance. No provider charges without exact free quota confirmation.
